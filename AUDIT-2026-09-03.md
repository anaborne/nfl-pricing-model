This repository was audited on 2026-09-03 against its own claims, over three rounds of
review. Eleven issues were found across the README and the three scripts, and all eleven
are closed in the tree this file ships in.

Issues 1 through 5 came from the first round. Issues 6 through 11 came from the second
round and are defects the first round's fixes introduced or left behind.

## 1. The stated mechanism for the loss

Wrong: the README said "The mechanism behind that gap is overconfidence, and not
directional bias", and closed the paragraph with "The ratings carry real signal, stated
with more certainty than the information behind them supports." Fitting the recalibration
and applying it shows overconfidence accounts for 8.1% of the Brier gap. Recalibrating
both forecasters leaves 93.0% of the gap standing.

Changed: the paragraph states that the model is mildly overconfident and that this
accounts for little of the gap, gives the recalibrated model Brier of 0.2229, the 8% and
93% shares, and names resolution as the remainder, with AUC of 0.720 for the market
against 0.687 for the model. `src/evaluate.py` computes all of those and writes them to
`output/discrimination.csv`.

## 2. Home-field advantage applied at neutral sites

Wrong: `+48` Elo points were added to the designated home team at every game, including
the games nflverse marks `location == Neutral`. The 2025 regular season has seven of
those, all international games.

Changed: `EloState.hfa` returns 0 at a neutral site, and both the pre-game probability in
`run_walkforward` and the rating update in `EloState.update` call it. Every one of the 272
2025 predictions moved, by at most 0.0695. Model Brier went from 0.223503 to 0.223911,
the paired Brier difference from +0.01190 to +0.01231, and the model's recalibration
slope from 0.889 to 0.877.

## 3. Scope of the sensitivity sweep

Wrong: the README said `src/sensitivity.py` "re-runs the whole walk-forward under a grid
of alternatives to each of these" after a list of five constants. The script varies three
of them.

Changed: the README names `K`, home-field advantage, season carryover and the two de-vig
alternatives, and states that the margin-of-victory dampener and the franchise-alias rule
are not swept. `output/sensitivity.csv` carries 17 rows across those four sweeps.

## 4. Moneyline coverage

Wrong: the README described `data/games.csv` as carrying "the recorded moneyline and
spread alongside final scores for every NFL game back to 1999". Moneylines first appear
in 2006, and the 2006 through 2009 seasons are partial, with 47, 1, 71 and 14 regular-season
games missing them.

Changed: the README says final scores and the spread back to 1999, and the recorded
moneyline from 2006 onward. Issue 8 covers the coverage figure itself.

## 5. Calibration bins described as deciles

Wrong: the README called the reliability-diagram bins "probability deciles".
`calibration_bins` builds its edges with `np.linspace(0, 1, 11)`, which is ten
fixed-width bins holding whatever number of games fall into each.

Changed: the README calls them fixed-width 0.1 probability bins and states that the bins
are equal width. The model's top occupied bin holds one game and the market's holds three.

## 6. Two confidence intervals for one statistic

Wrong: the Limitations section carried the pre-fix paired interval [+0.00175, +0.02205]
while the results table carried [+0.00217, +0.02245], so the README stated two different
95% CIs for the same statistic.

Changed: both read [+0.00217, +0.02245], matching `output/paired_test.csv`, where ci_low
is 0.002169823556969361 and ci_high is 0.022453336881880837.

## 7. Four figures no committed script produced

Wrong: the paragraph diagnosing the loss cited a recalibrated model Brier of 0.2229, an 8%
share of the gap closed, 93% of the gap left after recalibrating both forecasters, and AUC
of 0.720 against 0.687. `src/evaluate.py` fit the recalibration and wrote only the
intercept and slope to `output/recalibration.csv`. It never applied the fit, never scored
the recalibrated probabilities, and never computed an AUC. All four figures were correct
and none of them regenerated from a clean clone.

Changed: `src/evaluate.py` applies both fits, scores the recalibrated probabilities,
computes the share of the gap each recalibration closes, and computes both AUCs as the
Mann-Whitney statistic with ties split evenly. It prints them and writes
`output/discrimination.csv`, which the README cites. The committed output list grew from
12 files to 13.

## 8. Moneyline coverage off by one game

Wrong: the README said the moneyline is "complete from 2010". `2017_04_CHI_GB`, Chicago
at Green Bay in week 4 of 2017, has no `away_moneyline` and no `home_moneyline`.

Changed: the README says complete from 2010 apart from a single 2017 game. That game is
the only played regular-season game from 2010 onward missing either field.

## 9. Scripts failed after `output/` was deleted

Wrong: none of the three scripts created `output/`. After `rm -rf output`, the first write
raised `OSError`, so the README's Reproduce it block did not run on a tree where the
directory had been removed.

Changed: `os.makedirs("output", exist_ok=True)` runs at the top of each writer in
`src/elo_model.py`, `src/evaluate.py` and `src/sensitivity.py`. The three commands were
run in order against a deleted `output/` for this audit.

## 10. Discarded contrast in the bin description

Wrong: "The bins are equal width, not equal count."

Changed: "The bins are equal width. Each holds however many games fell into it, and the
marker at the top of the range stands on very few."

## 11. Banned word in the calibration paragraph

Wrong: "The market's 0.985 is essentially 1."

Changed: "The market's 0.985 is as close to 1 as this sample can show."

Model probabilities: the walk-forward was reimplemented from `data/games.csv` alone, in
code importing nothing from `src/`. The largest absolute difference against
`output/predictions_2025.csv` over all 272 games is 0.0.

Market probabilities: the moneyline conversion and the proportional de-vig were
reimplemented the same way. The largest absolute difference against the committed
`market_prob_home` is 0.0, and the mean overround is 0.042804882369.

Lookahead: flipping the result of the final 2025 game changes zero predictions. Flipping
the result of the game at index 136 of 272 changes zero predictions at or before it and 73
after it. Flipping a week 18 result changes zero predictions. Processing order is
calendar-monotone, with no adjacent pair in which the later-processed game was played on
an earlier date, and no season in which one week ends after the next begins. No team plays
twice on the same date, so reordering same-day games cannot move a rating a same-day game
is priced from. Re-running under strictly chronological order, which moves 4,855 of 6,495
games to a different position, changes no prediction and leaves model Brier at 0.223911.

Scored sample: the model probabilities, the market probabilities and the outcomes cover
the same 272 game ids, with no duplicates and no missing values in either probability
column. Both Brier series are computed over that one set. Independent recomputation gives
model 0.223911, market 0.211600 and naive 0.249223 on Brier, and 0.637991, 0.608230 and
0.691605 on log loss.

## What remains unverifiable

Three things about this repository cannot be settled from inside it. Whether the recorded
moneylines are opening or closing quotes is undocumented, since nflverse names no provider
and describes the fields only as "Odds for away/home team to win the game", so the size of
the gap against a real closing line is unknown, and the README says as much. The vendored
snapshot cannot be validated against its source, because `data/SOURCE.txt` records a fetch
at 2026-08-26T19:54:33Z, nflverse revises historical rows, and re-pulling today would not
establish what was served then. One season of 272 games also bounds very little: the
paired 95% CI on the Brier difference runs [+0.00217, +0.02245], and the model's
recalibration slope of 0.877138 carries a standard error of 0.1734, so a 95% interval on
that slope runs [0.537, 1.217] and includes 1, which means this sample cannot separate the
model's calibration from perfect. The 8% share of the gap attributed to calibration is a
point estimate with the same limit behind it. The direction of the result holds across the
whole sensitivity grid and across all three tie treatments, and this sample does not pin
down the magnitudes.
